The Sentry Voice
Sentry’s voice is informative, approachable, and understanding. We’re never condescending or mean.
Think of Sentry as the smart kid in the back of your engineering course: knows the answers, willing to help, never showing off. We poke fun at the material, not the teacher. And never at the customer, competitors, or any group of people.
We want readers to feel understood, not marketed to. They should be in on the joke, never the butt of it. Our humor is dry, self-aware, and concise, we say what everyone’s thinking.
We’re also:
Informative: We give real, useful answers. We don’t manipulate or overpromise.


Self-aware: We help developers find and fix bugs. We’re not “changing the world.”


Plainspoken: We use clear, American English. No jargon, idioms, or fancy words. Accessible to non-native speakers.


Unexcitable: Avoid all caps, exclamation points, or hype.


Fun: This stuff is dry, we make it interesting with deadpan humor and a conversational tone.


Tone
Confident and approachable, like talking to a peer. Tone shifts with context:
Introducing something new? Keep it light and fun.


Helping fix a problem? Be serious and empathetic.


Writing style
Every piece of content should do at least one of these:
Educate: Help users make decisions with the info they need.


Inform: Share updates on our product, company, or events.


Guide: Walk users through getting value from Sentry.


In doing so:
Simplify: Make technical topics easy to understand.


Respect: Speak to our users the way you’d speak to your friends, family, or favorite barista, and don’t patronize.


Be clear: Use simple, direct language.


Be relevant: Ask: Who is this for? How can this help them? What do they need to know?


Be entertaining: Make technical reading less boring, it should be fun to consume.


Blog posts should be clear to non-coders without patronizing those who are technical. Avoid personal anecdotes unless they serve the point.
Rules to remember
Start strong: Readers should know “this is for me” within two paragraphs.


Skip excitement about us: Never write “We’re excited to announce…” — readers don’t care how we feel.


Avoid repetition: Don’t reuse the same cliché, word, or joke.


Don’t tell people how to feel: Avoid hyperbole and “best ever” claims.


Write for people, not robots: SEO matters, but sounding human matters more.


Use American spelling when writing as Sentry.


Humor
Humor is core to Sentry. It makes our content relatable and fun, but never at the expense of clarity or respect. Jokes must have a purpose: to humanize, entertain, and build connection. It shouldn’t just fill space.
The Sentry Sense of Humor
Dry, self-aware, honest: Say what everyone’s thinking.


Respectful: Laugh with readers, never at them.


Straightforward: Jokes shouldn’t need explaining.


Dry, not sarcastic: Sarcasm risks misinterpretation.


Smart: High-ish brow, clear logic, and only good puns.


Original: No clichés, stolen jokes, or overused memes.


Self-aware: Light self-deprecation is fine; it should feel affectionate, not insecure.


Dos & Don’ts
Do
Play nice: avoid off-color or controversial topics (sex, politics, race, religion, gender, disability, etc.).


Keep jokes quick and punchy.


Make fun of actions, not people (the code is bad, not the developer).


Don’t
Be cringy or predictable.


Use overplayed references or forced humor.


Force a joke — if it’s not funny, skip it.
